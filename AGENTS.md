# AGENTS

## Vision

Expl is an agentic tool for exploring a problem space. As the successor to [k-kinzal/iter](https://github.com/k-kinzal/iter), it searches broadly, deeply, and in parallel—rather than committing to a single linear path—to uncover the strongest ways to achieve a goal, or the answer to a question.

Real problems rarely have a single answer. Expl models exploration as a directed graph of **hypotheses**—steps that might lead toward human-set goals—and drives it through one loop:

1. **Hypothesize**: enumerate the possibilities a goal opens up, broadly and deeply, as hypotheses.
2. **Plan**: assemble them into paths—hypotheses built on hypotheses—and rank which are most promising.
3. **Verify** them, most promising first.
4. **Evaluate** each verification from its result: whether the hypothesis held—done, abandoned, or to be continued—and how far it moves each goal.
5. **Keep going**: unless a human stops it, look at the other paths too.

- **Multiple solutions and goals.** Depending on a goal's level of abstraction, several distinct approaches may satisfy it, and multiple goals can be pursued side by side.
- **Branching and merging.** Competing ideas diverge along separate paths, while complementary steps can merge (`A -> C` and `B -> C`) into a single downstream step.
- **Forward and backward search.** Exploration proposes steps forward from what already exists toward a goal and, when enabled for a goal, can also work backward via *counter-exploration* to identify what must hold just before the goal is met.
- **Wide frame.** An exploration handler can search in three ways, with subscriptions and routing deciding when each perspective runs: from a particular node toward what looks most promising for the goal; from a particular node toward what does not—niche directions inside the goal's frame that nobody asked for, and counterexamples that would work against the goal, so the map also shows what not to do; and from the whole graph at once, looking for what no single node would suggest—gaps between paths, combinations of separate paths, patterns across failures, assumptions every path shares. Exploration enumerates; planning ranks. Unlikely or counterproductive hypotheses are ranked low rather than discarded, and only duplicates are dropped. Exploration is never held back by how many hypotheses are waiting: a new hypothesis can change which path is most promising, and if many hypotheses slow progress down, that is for planning to solve. A starting point is never exhausted by one exploration, so expl returns to known nodes and keeps growing new hypotheses from them—including, where configured, steps not verified yet, explored on the assumption that they hold, so the map can grow deep before anything runs.
- **Subgoals.** Exploring a problem space is exploring uncertainty: a cycle of forming hypotheses and testing them—test the most promising, and when it fails, test another. A single step is too small a unit to reason about that cycle, so goals can have **subgoals**: hypotheses about a stage or precondition on the way to a goal ("if A held, the goal would be within reach"), which can have subgoals of their own. Subgoals are set by people—the human, or the AI overseeing the exploration—never generated by exploration, because choosing the propositions worth testing is the job of whoever shapes the exploration; they often become visible only after some exploration. A goal does not require its subgoals: several subgoals of one goal are alternative routes (reaching A or B may be enough, and reaching A does not rule out pursuing B), so they order the work rather than gate it. Like everything else they are judged, not decided once: a subgoal that looks unreachable can become reachable as other work lands, so reaching or missing one is never final. Exploration stays free: a step may head for a subgoal, for the goal directly, or for neither in particular, because subgoals are hypotheses too.
- **Failure is information.** A step is a hypothesis, and not every hypothesis survives verification: code may not come together, a check may not pass, a question may turn out unanswerable. A failed step stays in the graph with what went wrong, and exploration grows new hypotheses from it—alternatives that avoid the cause, smaller steps, investigations into why—so a failure widens the search instead of ending a path.

Steps come in two kinds, by how their hypothesis is verified, and a graph can hold both:

- **Change steps** are verified by realizing them as concrete code: each one is committed on its own Git branch, and the work converges on the **trunk** when a human adopts it.
- **Investigation steps** change nothing. They are verified by answering a question about the code or the world—"is this repository affected by CVE-XXXX-YYYY?", "does any caller rely on this behaviour?"—and record a **finding**: the answer (yes, no, or unknown) with its evidence. They produce no commit and never reach the trunk; their findings converge on the **goal** they serve. An investigation goal is answered when the findings along a path settle it; evaluation records how far they look to.

Expl drives this graph through a continuous cycle of **exploration**, **planning**, **verification**, and **evaluation**. Exploration proposes hypotheses; planning assembles them into paths—hierarchies in which each hypothesis builds on the ones before it—joins them, and sets their priorities; verification tests ready hypotheses, highest priority first (committing change steps on Git branches, recording the findings of investigation steps); and evaluation reads each verification's result—whether the hypothesis held: done, abandoned, or to be continued—and how far it moves each active goal, feeding every outcome back into further exploration.

Finding one passing path never ends the search, because better, simpler, or contradicting alternatives may still exist. Expl's job is to keep exploring, generating and verifying hypotheses, each with evaluation's reading of how far it meets each goal; the human's job is to define the goals, add hypotheses of their own whenever they see one (from the CLI, like any other node), and decide which hypotheses to adopt. Whoever defines a goal also decides whether it is truly met: the goal states what meeting it takes—"proved in Lean", "the test suite passes"—and evaluation's verdicts are information toward that decision.

Expl follows the **trunk**, the default branch declared in `expl.toml`. Humans adopt verified change steps by merging them into it with git, resolving conflicts as with any merge. Exploration always starts again from where the trunk stands. Earlier positions are never discarded: every origin the trunk has held stays in the graph together with what was adopted into it, so the history of choices stays visible. Hypotheses left behind on an older origin can still be taken in later.

## Runtime and Deployment

`expl_runtime` is the framework. It owns what every explorer needs: the event log and its delivery (leases, retries, priorities, routing, concurrency limits), the workers that deliver, the daemon, agent commands, and Git clones. An **explorer** is an application of the framework. It keeps its own state beside the event log, publishes events, and **registers** its consumers: for each, which events it reacts to, the command executed for one event, and where that command runs. `expl_core` is the general-purpose explorer, one such application. It owns the graph and the exploration, planning, verification, and evaluation handlers, and registers them from `expl.toml`. A specialized explorer is another application of the same framework, with events, state, and handlers of its own. Neither builds on the other.

A worker belongs to the framework and holds no application code. It leases one event at a time for a registered consumer, executes the registered command for it, keeps the lease alive while the command runs, and settles the delivery from the command's outcome.

A **daemon** hosts every explorer registered with it and drives them from their registrations. It elects a leader through the cluster's shared storage. The leader starts the registered consumers' workers and, after an election, restarts them from the same durable state. Members accept operations and forward them to the leader, and each member can execute commands in private Git clones. The leader also executes commands, so a standalone daemon fills both roles. The daemon controls the agents of all its explorers: its operator defines agent commands and sets how many may run at once, in total and for each pool of explorers. When `[workspace] remote` is configured, the Git remote supplies the trunk and receives committed hypothesis branches. Humans still move the trunk with Git.

## Core Modeling

Humans and AI agents collaborate in Expl through one shared mental model anchored in a Git repository. Both sides read and extend the same **graph** of hypotheses, with **events** turning human intent and agent outcomes into asynchronous work:

- **Graph** is the shared map of the exploration. It holds **origins** (positions of the trunk; the newest, *active* one is where exploration starts), **steps** (hypotheses, each a unit of work: *change* steps or *investigation* steps), and **goals** (objectives or questions set by a human, each a separate perspective the work is judged from; a goal can be a *subgoal* of others, a hypothesis about a route toward them). Directed edges record which nodes build on others, which goals a step heads toward, and which goals a subgoal serves, so paths can branch and join. Humans can add any node from the CLI, not only goals.
- **Trunk** is the default branch (`trunk` in `expl.toml`). Only humans move it, and they do so with git. Expl never writes to it; it follows it. Every running project worker watches the trunk's tip, and when the tip moves it records a new origin (`origin.advanced`) that builds on the previous origin and on the steps the trunk took in. The previous origin becomes *superseded*, and that chain is the **lineage**. Git is the ground truth for **adoption**: a completed change step is adopted when the trunk contains its commits, either by history (merge or fast-forward) or by patch (squash merge or cherry-pick, detected with `git cherry`). Steps keep their base when the trunk moves: whether a step that has not started is moved onto the new origin, kept on the code it was proposed for, or dropped is planning's call, because not every step still makes sense on newer code. Investigation steps are never adopted; what they found stays on the node and counts toward their goal.
- **Workspace** is where an agent runs: a private clone of the repository with its whole history, in which the agent uses git as freely as any other tool. What the agent does with git stays in its clone; the project's refs, the trunk among them, move only by expl and by humans. Exploration, planning, and evaluation run in scratch clones at the relevant commit. Each change step has its own worktree, which only expl touches: when paths join, the step starts there by merging its predecessors' branches, and the agent works in a clone of it. Once the agent is done—finished or failed—expl takes in the files the clone holds and commits them to the step's branch before evaluation looks at it, so every step derived from it starts from that commit. Paths the declaration freezes (`[workspace] frozen` in `expl.toml`) stay as they were in that commit, so the agent can read and try anything while the result holds only what the exploration allows. An investigation step runs in a scratch clone at the code it is about and leaves no commit behind.
- **Event** is an immutable record of something that happened in the graph, such as `goal.added` or `node.ready`. Human commands, trunk followers, and handlers produce events. Graph changes and their events are recorded together.
- **Consumer** subscribes to named events in `expl.toml` and declares what happens when one arrives. Consumers can process the same event independently, or a router can choose one recipient from a delivery group. The handler defines the graph operation, the workspace, and the response that can be applied to the graph. The command and driver define how to get that response. An agent CLI or an algorithm can supply it through that contract.
  - **Exploration** proposes new hypotheses from the active origin, from known nodes revisited at random, from steps assumed to hold, from failures, backward from counter-exploration goals, or across the whole graph.
  - **Planning** assembles hypotheses into paths, prioritizes them, joins converging paths, prunes duplicates, and decides which unstarted steps move onto a new origin.
  - **Verification** tests ready hypotheses, highest priority first, and records a commit for a change step or a finding for an investigation.
  - **Evaluation** records whether a verified hypothesis is done, abandoned, or to be continued. It judges how far the step's path or a new origin moves each active goal. The resulting events feed further exploration and planning.
- **Process** is a worker for a consumer. It leases one event at a time from the durable inbox, executes the consumer's reaction as a command, and settles the delivery from its outcome. Replicas share deliveries and process independent events in parallel. A lease coordinates work about the same node within a consumer and lets another replica recover an interrupted delivery.

Together, these primitives close the loop between human intent and AI autonomy: humans set **goals**, add hypotheses, and merge the **hypotheses** evaluated as satisfying a goal (change steps) into the trunk or read the **findings** that answer a goal, while **consumers** handle **events** using isolated **workspaces** and record outcomes that feed further exploration.
